# The Gold Table image direction

## The role of photography

Every image should make a seller feel that a local event is calm, private and easy to attend. A venue manager should see a respectful, compact operation that fits their space. The photography is evidence of a clear human process, not decoration and not a luxury-jewellery campaign.

## Visual system

- **Mood:** candid British event-day reportage; warm, measured, practical and reassuring.
- **Palette:** The Gold Table navy, soft cream, antique gold, warm timber and natural venue light.
- **People:** genuine-looking multigenerational local customers and a visibly diverse team. Let people look engaged and at ease; avoid posed smiles or surprise reactions.
- **Places:** pub private rooms, golf clubs, hotel rooms and community halls. Each must feel recognisably different while using the same calm consultation setup.
- **Objects:** simple trays, loupes, scales, pouches and jewellery boxes. Show only as much jewellery as clarifies the process. Never show cash.
- **Exclusions:** legible customer information, printed promises, prominent logos, luxury-retail sets, crowded queues, staged deal handshakes, implausible jewellery or AI-looking hands.

## Brief for a real event-day photographer

### Shoot approach

Work as a quiet documentary photographer. Arrive before doors open to photograph the room and setup, then spend most of the day near real conversations without interrupting them. Shoot with natural light where possible; use a subtle bounced fill only when essential. Keep the scene believable rather than over-styled.

### Required shot list

1. One calm, horizontal hero consultation with clean space for website copy.
2. A vertical close-up of testing or explanation: customer, valuer and item all remain understandable.
3. One wider contextual image for each venue type: pub, golf club, hotel and community hall.
4. At least two venue-partnership images: manager and coordinator inspecting the room/setup.
5. A hands-only at-home preparation image, shot separately with a consented contributor.
6. A simple mail-pack still life only after that service, packaging and legal wording are approved.
7. Useful supporting frames: welcome, discreet waiting area, accessibility route, table preparation, tea/refreshments and closing down.

### Operational and consent rules

- Obtain written image consent before anyone becomes identifiable; maintain a no-photo option and shoot around it.
- Never photograph ID, payment details, valuation paperwork, addresses or customer collections in a way that can identify ownership.
- Do not direct anyone to sell, to pretend to react to an offer, or to display cash.
- Deliver a contact sheet first, then provide a selected set in large WebP/AVIF and archive-quality originals. Include landscape, portrait and crop-safe variants.
- Keep a release record tied to every delivered image before it is published.

## Pre-publication image check

- Does this make attendance feel simple, personal and low-pressure?
- Does the venue look orderly and easy to host?
- Does the setting match the event page it appears on?
- Are there no unverified claims, private details, cash, misleading signage or missing consents?
- Does the crop work on both a phone and desktop without hiding the human moment?
